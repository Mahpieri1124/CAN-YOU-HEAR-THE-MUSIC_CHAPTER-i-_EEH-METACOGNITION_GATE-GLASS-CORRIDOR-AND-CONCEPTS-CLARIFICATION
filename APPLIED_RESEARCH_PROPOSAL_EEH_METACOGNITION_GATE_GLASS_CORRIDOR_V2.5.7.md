## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal develops Version 2.5.7 into two connected tracks.

**Track A — EEH-METACOGNITION-GATE:** a real-time human metacognitive filter for AI-assisted research.

**Track B — Project Glass Corridor:** a humanitarian application domain for studying risk-sensitive transparency, independent verification, multi-source observation, human oversight, and controlled exit capacity.

The central proposition is:

> **Not every valid AI-generated challenge should become the next research problem.**

EEH-GATE evaluates each significant challenge through two criteria: impact on the core claim and impact on feasibility/applicability. The researcher chooses **CONTINUE, STOP, or REDIRECT / RESTRUCTURE**.

## 2. Central Research Question

> **Can a disciplined human metacognitive gate improve the quality of AI-assisted research trajectory decisions by identifying early which AI-generated challenges are central, which are peripheral, which require restructuring, and which should be stopped or deferred?**

## 3. Secondary Questions

1. Does EEH-GATE reduce unnecessary research-branch expansion?
2. Does it reduce time spent on technically valid but non-central problems?
3. Does it improve early recognition of threats to the core claim?
4. Does the dual assessment improve decision quality?
5. Does explicit Continue / Stop / Redirect preserve research coherence?
6. Can EEH-GATE reduce technical LOOPTIMA?
7. Can the same architecture be transferred to high-consequence humanitarian applications?

## 4. Glass Corridor Application Question

> **Can a risk-sensitive humanitarian passage architecture combine appropriate transparency, multi-source observation, independent verification, digital provenance, human oversight, and controlled exit capacity to reduce selected forms of uncertainty and coordination failure without creating false confidence in safety?**

## 6. Track A — Research-Triage Study

### Phase A1 — Scenario Construction

Develop controlled research scenarios containing a central research question, AI-generated outputs, valid central objections, valid peripheral objections, feasibility problems, implementation details, and distracting technical branches.

No real-world high-risk operation is required for this phase.

### Phase A2 — Baseline

Participants perform research-triage tasks without a formal EEH-GATE. Record branch proliferation, time spent, technical detours, central-question retention, and final decisions.

### Phase A3 — EEH Condition

Participants use:

```text
CORE CLAIM IMPACT?
        +
FEASIBILITY / APPLICABILITY IMPACT?
        ↓
CONTINUE / STOP / REDIRECT
```

Compare the results with baseline.

## 7. Candidate Hypotheses

**H1 — Branch Reduction:** EEH-GATE reduces unnecessary branch proliferation.

**H2 — Centrality Preservation:** EEH-GATE improves retention of the original question.

**H3 — Earlier Failure Detection:** EEH-GATE improves early recognition of central threats.

**H4 — Feasibility Detection:** EEH-GATE improves recognition of practical infeasibility.

**H5 — Exit Capacity:** EEH-GATE increases timely stopping or restructuring.

**H6 — Reduced Technical LOOPTIMA:** EEH-GATE reduces disproportionate investment in non-central technical subproblems.

**H7 — Human–AI Complementarity:** AI expansion of the search space becomes more productive when humans explicitly control trajectory selection.

## 8. Candidate Metrics

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Branch Proliferation Index | number of new branches per core question |
| Central Question Retention | degree of alignment with the original objective |
| Decision Latency | time from challenge appearance to classification |
| LOOPTIMA Exposure | time invested in non-central technical branches |
| Redirect Accuracy | appropriateness of restructuring decisions |
| Stop Accuracy | appropriateness of stopping decisions |
| Continue Accuracy | appropriateness of continuation decisions |
| Feasibility Detection | early identification of practical failure |
| AI-Induced Drift | trajectory change attributable to AI output |
| Exit Capacity | ability to stop/restructure before excessive investment |

These are candidate metrics, not validated instruments.

## 9. Track B — Glass Corridor

Glass Corridor should initially be studied through conceptual modelling, legal/humanitarian review, failure-surface analysis, simulation, evidence/provenance modelling, and independent expert review.

The project must not move directly from concept to deployment.

## 10. Vulnerable-Civilian Module

A priority scenario is potential voluntary humanitarian passage involving infants, children, severely malnourished children, wounded or sick persons, people requiring urgent medical care, caregivers, older persons, and persons with disabilities.

The scenario must remain a decision problem rather than an assumption that evacuation is always safer.

Relevant humanitarian law and practice already address protection and evacuation in besieged/encircled settings; Glass Corridor does not create those rights. The ICRC emphasizes voluntary choice, family unity, adequate reception conditions, and the continuing hazards of safe-passage operations. citeturn0search0turn0search7turn0search8

Personal data should be minimized. Public accountability does not require public disclosure of every identity or medical record.

## 12. Risk-Sensitive Transparency Model

### Layer 1 — Public Accountability
Information necessary for legitimate public scrutiny.

### Layer 2 — Independent Verification
Information available to appropriately independent verification actors.

### Layer 3 — Humanitarian Coordination / Safety
Information required for legitimate coordination and safety.

### Layer 4 — Protected Information
Information whose disclosure could create unacceptable risk.

> **Maximum useful verifiability with minimum unnecessary exposure.**

## 13. Human–AI Monitoring

AI may support anomaly detection, cross-source comparison, inconsistent-record detection, uncertainty flagging, environmental-change detection, documentation, and audit support.

AI must not independently determine humanitarian eligibility, whether a child should be moved, whether a movement is ethically justified, or whether a high-risk movement should continue.

## 14. Reverse Risk Engineering

Candidate failure classes include:

### Human
misjudgment, fatigue, overconfidence, responsibility overload.

### Information
false information, contradictory reports, manipulated metadata, incomplete records.

### Technical
sensor failure, camera failure, communication failure, synchronization failure.

### Adversarial
spoofing, deception, manipulation, deliberate information leakage.

### Humanitarian
family separation, medical deterioration, unsafe destination, loss of consent.

### Governance
institutional failure, ambiguous authority, inadequate independent verification.

## 15. EXIT Architecture

The system must preserve:

**STOP / HOLD / REROUTE / ABORT / REASSESS**

Exit is a safety property, not a failure of the architecture.

## 16. ZERO STATE / ABSOLUTE ZERO TIME Simulation

A dedicated simulation class can examine decreasing decision time combined with increasing uncertainty, fear, information volume, restriction, and consequence.

The goal is to examine whether metacognitive filtering degrades nonlinearly under extreme conditions. This remains a conceptual model until operationalized and empirically tested.

## 17. UUP-INFO-FLOW Simulation

A second simulation can model an information stream characterized by novelty, uncertainty, contradiction, incomplete observability, high emotional salience, and high perceived consequence.

Candidate outcomes include delayed decision, panic, freezing, incorrect prioritization, excessive delegation, or premature action.

The objective is to study information-processing failure conditions, not to label people clinically.

## 18. Research Governance

Every major new result should pass through EEH-GATE:

```text
NEW RESULT
   ↓
DOES IT CHANGE THE CORE CLAIM?
   ↓
DOES IT CHANGE FEASIBILITY?
   ↓
CONTINUE / STOP / REDIRECT
```

A result that does not materially change the central question should not automatically create a new research programme.

## 19. Anti-LOOPTIMA Test

Before adding a technical subsystem, ask:

1. Does it affect the core claim?
2. Does it improve a measurable research outcome?
3. Does it reduce a demonstrated failure mode?
4. Does it preserve or improve exit capacity?
5. Is its complexity justified?
6. Can it be removed without changing the central proposition?

If predominantly negative, defer or reject the addition.

## 20. Ethical Gate

Any future research involving humans must pass appropriate legal, humanitarian, technical, and ethics review and must prioritize child safeguarding, privacy, data minimization, lawful consent where applicable, independent oversight, and explicit abort criteria.

No vulnerable population should be exposed to experimental risk merely because the theoretical question is important.

## 21. Operational Boundary

This proposal is not an operational field manual. It does not prescribe military communications, tactical routes, aircraft specifications, sensor frequencies, evasion methods, defensive systems, weapons, intelligence procedures, or conflict-specific operational instructions.

## 23. Expected Contributions

### Conceptual
A clarified distinction between HMC-GATE and EEH-METACOGNITION-GATE.

### Methodological
A simple dual-axis decision mechanism for rapid research-trajectory filtering.

### Systems
A formal relationship between metacognitive filtering and LOOPTIMA prevention.

### Human–AI
A division of labor in which AI expands information while humans retain trajectory-selection authority.

### Humanitarian
A framework for investigating whether observability, verification, and human oversight can reduce selected uncertainty around civilian passage.

## 24. Limitations

The proposal cannot guarantee correct human judgment, absence of bias, successful humanitarian passage, protection from hostile action, truth from sensors, immunity from manipulation, or elimination of uncertainty.

EEH-GATE can itself become a LOOPTIMA if its rules become rigid and prevent legitimate exploration.

> **The gate must itself remain subject to metacognitive review.**

## 25. Meta-Gate

```text
EEH-GATE
   ↓
DECISION
   ↓
OUTCOME
   ↓
WAS THE GATE ITSELF CORRECT?
   ↓
REASSESS THE GATE
```

This recursive property prevents the gate from becoming an unquestionable authority.

## 26. Final Research Propositions

> **EEH-METACOGNITION-GATE proposes that human researchers can use a disciplined, real-time metacognitive filter after significant AI outputs to distinguish challenges that threaten the core claim or feasibility of a research trajectory from challenges that should be deferred, bounded, or rejected as unnecessary diversions.**

> **Glass Corridor proposes that risk-sensitive transparency, multi-source observation, independent verification, digital provenance, human oversight, and controlled exit capacity may reduce selected forms of uncertainty and coordination failure during humanitarian civilian movement, without constituting a guarantee of safety.**

Both remain research hypotheses.

## 28. Central Principle

> **The purpose of EEH-METACOGNITION-GATE is not to make the human faster than AI.**
>
> **Its purpose is to keep the human responsible for deciding what should matter.**

And for Glass Corridor:

> **The purpose of the Glass Corridor is not to make war transparent.**
>
> **The purpose is to make humanitarian movement as observable, verifiable, accountable and human-supervised as safely possible.**
