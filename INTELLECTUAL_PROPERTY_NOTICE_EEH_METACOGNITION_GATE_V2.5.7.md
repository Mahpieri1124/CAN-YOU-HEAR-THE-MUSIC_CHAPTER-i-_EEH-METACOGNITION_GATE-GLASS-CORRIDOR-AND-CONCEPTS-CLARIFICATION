## 7. Glass Corridor — No Guarantee of Safety

The project rejects the interpretation:

> **“Glass Corridor guarantees safe passage.”**

The intended proposition is narrower:

> **A risk-sensitive humanitarian passage architecture may reduce selected forms of uncertainty, identification ambiguity, information asymmetry, and coordination failure.**

Therefore:

- visibility ≠ safety;
- transparency ≠ immunity;
- deconfliction ≠ protection;
- observation ≠ truth;
- AI assistance ≠ human responsibility;
- humanitarian declaration ≠ guaranteed safety.

## 8. Risk-Sensitive Transparency

“Glass” does not mean unrestricted disclosure. Public accountability must be balanced with privacy, child protection, medical confidentiality, operational safety, and protection against information leakage.

> **Transparency should increase verifiability without unnecessarily increasing operational vulnerability.**

## 9. Evidence Boundary

> **Live video is evidence, not truth by itself.**

> **Distributed observation ≠ distributed truth.**

Cameras, telemetry, metadata, manifests, human reports, and AI outputs can fail, be incomplete, or be manipulated. Independent verification and human interpretation remain necessary.

## 10. Human–AI Responsibility

AI may assist with comparison, anomaly detection, documentation, uncertainty identification, and audit support. It is not granted autonomous authority over high-consequence humanitarian decisions.

EEH-GATE is a human-controlled mechanism and is not claimed to be infallible.

## 11. Anti-LOOPTIMA Governance

A valid criticism does not automatically become a new research branch. A technically sophisticated challenge can be real while remaining peripheral to the central research question.

The governance rule is:

> **Not every valid challenge becomes an active research branch.**

The gate itself must remain open to reassessment so that it does not become a rigid LOOPTIMA.
